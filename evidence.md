# evidence.md — Smart Sub Tracker

> Key insights from user research, used to ground product.md (Strategy and Scope).

---

## 1. Research Goal

Understand how people of different ages, incomes and life situations keep track of their recurring subscriptions. Find out where they lose money or control, what stops them from cancelling, and what would make them trust an app with their subscription and payment data.

**Research questions**
1. How do people currently know what they're subscribed to and what it costs?
2. What triggers them to cancel, or regret not cancelling, a subscription?
3. How do they handle free trials, price increases and shared plans?
4. How willing are they to share bank or card data, and what would they need to trust it?
5. Would they want suggestions for cheaper alternatives?

## 2. Method

- **Format:** 8 semi-structured interviews, 10–15 minutes each, on 04.11.2026
- **Approach:** synthetic persona interviews. Participant profiles were defined by the team in a workshop session; the transcripts were generated with AI assistance to simulate how each persona would answer. They are not real people, and the findings must be validated with real users before product decisions are final (see §9 Limitations).
- **Sampling:** deliberately diverse: students, workers, a founder, a retiree and a parent; ages 20–68; lower to upper-middle income
- **Note on currency:** all amounts are monthly, in euros

### Interview guide

1. Walk me through every subscription you pay for right now.
2. Roughly how much do you spend on them per month?
3. When did you last pay for something you'd forgotten about, or didn't use?
4. Tell me about the last free trial you signed up for.
5. Have you noticed a price increase this year? How did you find out?
6. Do you share any plans? How do you split the cost?
7. Tell me about the last time you tried to cancel something.
8. How would you feel about an app reading your bank transactions to find subscriptions?
9. If you had one magic button for your subscriptions, what would it do?

## 3. Interview Transcripts

*Shortened. I = interviewer.*

### P1 — Nir A., 23, student · 7 subs · €90/month

**I:** Walk me through your subscriptions.
**Nir:** Spotify student, Netflix, Amazon Prime, iCloud, a VPN, a language app… and ChatGPT Plus, I think. Maybe something else. Seven? Could be eight.
**I:** How much is that per month?
**Nir:** Honestly I'd have guessed sixty. If you say ninety I'd believe it, but it would hurt.
**I:** Last free trial?
**Nir:** A fitness app. Seven-day trial, then it charged me €59.99 for a whole year. I only noticed because my bank app said I was low on money.
**I:** Did you get it back?
**Nir:** I emailed them, no answer. I just gave up.
**I:** Bank connection?
**Nir:** If it's read-only and from a known company, maybe. I wouldn't give my login to a random app.
**I:** Magic button?
**Nir:** One alert the day *before* a trial turns into a payment. Not after.

### P2 — Arthur B., 20, ESCP student · 7 subs · €132/month

**I:** What are you subscribed to?
**Arthur:** Spotify Family with my siblings, Netflix Premium, Disney+, a gym, Uber One, Apple Music because I forgot to cancel it when I switched to Spotify, and a news app.
**I:** You pay for two music services?
**Arthur:** Yeah. For about four months now. That's the problem: I only see it on my card statement in lump sums, and I don't read those.
**I:** Price increases?
**Arthur:** Netflix went up again. I saw an email title, didn't open it. I only realised when my statement looked higher.
**I:** How does Spotify Family work for you?
**Arthur:** I pay, my two siblings are supposed to send me their share. One always forgets. I don't want to chase my little sister for €4.
**I:** Bank connection?
**Arthur:** Fine. I already use Revolut, it does some of this, but not well. It doesn't tell me if something's cheaper elsewhere.
**I:** Magic button?
**Arthur:** "Show me what I'm paying twice for."

### P3 — Glenn T., 23, PhD student · 8 subs · €120/month

**I:** Your subscriptions?
**Glenn:** Mostly work-ish: Overleaf, Zotero storage, a reference manager, Google One, Adobe for figures. Then Netflix, Spotify, and a meal-kit box.
**I:** Do you know the total?
**Glenn:** Yes, I have a spreadsheet. But I update it maybe twice a year, so it's always wrong.
**I:** Last forgotten payment?
**Glenn:** Adobe renewed yearly. €290 at once, in the month my stipend was late. The yearly ones are the dangerous ones. You forget they exist.
**I:** Shared plans?
**Glenn:** Netflix with my two flatmates. We use Splitwise, but it doesn't remind anyone, so it's chaos.
**I:** Bank connection?
**Glenn:** I'd rather upload a statement once than give permanent access. Permanent access feels like too much.
**I:** Magic button?
**Glenn:** Warn me about yearly renewals a few weeks ahead, not a few days.

### P4 — Roberto, 48, construction worker · 5 subs · €200/month

**I:** What do you subscribe to?
**Roberto:** Football. That's it. But football is now in five places: one for the Bundesliga, one for the Champions League, one for the Premier League, one for the cup games, and one for Spanish football because I'm from Spain.
**I:** €200 a month?
**Roberto:** More or less. It's a lot for me. My wife says it's more than our electricity.
**I:** Have prices changed?
**Roberto:** Every season. They go up and I don't know until the money is gone.
**I:** Have you tried to cancel?
**Roberto:** In summer, when there's no football, I want to pause. But one of them has a 12-month contract. I didn't know. I had to pay it anyway.
**I:** Bank connection?
**Roberto:** No. I don't trust apps with my bank. I'd type it in myself if it's simple.
**I:** Magic button?
**Roberto:** Tell me what's the cheapest way to watch all my games. A package, anything.

### P5 — Helena S., 28, marketing coordinator · 12 subs · €145/month

**I:** How many subscriptions do you have?
**Helena:** I said eight on the sticky note, but I'm counting now… gym, a yoga studio, Netflix, Prime, Disney+, Spotify, Audible, a dating app, a skincare box, a meditation app, a photo-editing app, Dropbox. Twelve.
**I:** Which do you actually use?
**Helena:** Honestly? Yoga, Spotify, Netflix. The gym I go to maybe twice a month. I pay €40 for that.
**I:** Why not cancel the gym?
**Helena:** It's a German gym contract. Cancellation in writing, three months' notice, before the end of the minimum term. I missed the date last year, so it auto-extended for another year.
**I:** Free trials?
**Helena:** Audible started as a trial. I've been paying for eleven months and have three unread books.
**I:** Bank connection?
**Helena:** If they explain exactly what they see and I can delete it any time, yes.
**I:** Magic button?
**Helena:** Write the cancellation letter for me, with the right date, and remind me when to send it.

### P6 — Joe, 35, tech start-up CEO · 20 subs · €95 personal + ~€1,400 business/month

**I:** What are you subscribed to?
**Joe:** Personally, the usual: Spotify, Netflix, a newspaper, a password manager. Business is the real issue: Slack, Notion, Figma, GitHub, Google Workspace, a CRM, analytics tools, and three or four AI platforms where we pay per token.
**I:** Do you know the total?
**Joe:** For the company, my finance person knows at month-end. In real time, no. The AI token bills especially: one month €300, the next €900.
**I:** Paying for things you don't use?
**Joe:** Seats. We had six Figma seats for people who left months ago.
**I:** Bank connection?
**Joe:** Totally fine, we already connect everything to our accounting tool.
**I:** Magic button?
**Joe:** One dashboard showing per-tool spend and unused seats, with alerts when usage-based costs spike.

### P7 — Harold B., 68, retired accountant · 9 subs · €120/month

**I:** Your subscriptions?
**Harold:** The newspaper, print and digital, a magazine, Netflix my grandson set up, Amazon Prime, a wine club, a streaming service for classical concerts, a cloud backup, my phone's extra storage, and a charity membership.
**I:** Do you track them?
**Harold:** I was an accountant, so yes: a paper notebook. But the digital ones charge through Apple or Amazon, and the statement only says "APPLE.COM/BILL". I can't tell what's what.
**I:** Have you tried to cancel one online?
**Harold:** The magazine. I clicked through five pages of "Are you sure?" offers and still don't know if it worked. I ended up sending a letter.
**I:** Bank connection?
**Harold:** Absolutely not. I've had phishing attempts. I would never give an app my banking login.
**I:** Magic button?
**Harold:** A clear list in large print, and a proper letter I can print and post to cancel.

### P8 — Vincent F., 48, logistics manager, father of 3 · 3 subs (actual: 6) · €23 (actual: €52)

**I:** What are you subscribed to?
**Vincent:** Not much. Netflix, Spotify Family, and iCloud for the family photos. Around €23.
**I:** Any subscriptions on your kids' devices?
**Vincent:** …Let me check. Oh. A game pass on my son's console, something called "Gems Club" on my daughter's tablet, and a music-learning app. All on my card. That's another €29.
**I:** You didn't know about those?
**Vincent:** No. They're in-app purchases through the family account. I approved them at some point, I suppose, but I didn't realise they were monthly.
**I:** How do you feel about that?
**Vincent:** Annoyed. Not about the money as such; I just want to know what my kids sign up for.
**I:** Bank connection?
**Vincent:** Maybe, if it's a bank-approved service. I'd prefer uploading a statement.
**I:** Magic button?
**Vincent:** Group everything by family member so I see who is spending what.

## 4. Raw Findings

Grouped by theme (affinity mapping).

**Visibility and underestimation**
- P1 guessed €60 and was actually at €90.
- P5 put 8 on her persona card; the actual count was 12.
- P8 thought he had 3 subscriptions for €23; the actual was 6 for €52.
- P7 can't identify charges that appear as "APPLE.COM/BILL".
- P3 keeps a spreadsheet but it's always outdated.

**Forgotten payments and free trials**
- P1 had a 7-day trial auto-convert to a €59.99 yearly charge.
- P5 has been paying for Audible for 11 months after a trial.
- P3 was hit by a €290 yearly Adobe renewal; yearly renewals are "the dangerous ones".
- P2 paid for two music services for 4 months.
- P8's kids have in-app subscriptions he didn't know about.

**Cancellation friction**
- P5's gym needs written cancellation with 3 months' notice; she missed it and it auto-extended a year.
- P4 couldn't pause a sports subscription for summer because of a 12-month minimum term.
- P7 went through 5 retention screens, gave up and sent a letter.

**Price increases**
- P2 and P4 only noticed price increases on their statements or when the money was gone.
- P4: prices go up "every season".

**Shared plans**
- P2 doesn't want to chase siblings for €4.
- P3 uses Splitwise with flatmates, but there are no reminders.
- P8 wants spend grouped by family member.

**Trust and data access**
- Will connect a bank: P2, P6.
- Only with conditions (read-only, known provider, deletable, or statement upload instead): P1, P3, P5, P8.
- Refuses: P4, P7, citing phishing and distrust.

**Value and alternatives**
- P5 uses only 3 of her 12 subscriptions regularly.
- P4 wants the cheapest bundle for his sports.
- P2 wants to see duplicates and cheaper options.
- P6 has unused paid seats and unpredictable AI token costs.

## 5. Key Insights

Format: **persona** wants to **action** because **need**, but **friction**.

**Insight 1: Visibility**
A student or parent with several subscriptions wants to see all of them and the real monthly total in one place, because they want to know where their money goes. But charges are spread across cards, app stores and family accounts and labelled vaguely, so they underestimate both count and cost.
*Evidence: P1, P5, P7, P8 (6 of 8 underestimated).*

**Insight 2: Trials and yearly renewals**
A user who signs up for a free trial or a yearly plan wants to decide before money leaves their account, because a surprise charge hits hardest on a tight budget. But reminders come too late, or not at all, and yearly renewals are forgotten completely.
*Evidence: P1, P3, P5.*

**Insight 3: Cancellation friction**
A user who has decided to cancel wants to do it quickly and correctly, because otherwise the contract silently extends. But German contracts often require written notice before a deadline, and online flows hide the cancel button behind retention offers.
*Evidence: P4, P5, P7.*

**Insight 4: Silent price increases**
A long-term subscriber wants to know when a price goes up, because they'd re-evaluate whether it's still worth it. But increases are announced in easily ignored emails, so they only notice after paying.
*Evidence: P2, P3, P4, P7.*

**Insight 5: Trust vs. convenience**
Users want subscriptions detected automatically, because manual entry is tedious. But half are uncomfortable giving an app permanent bank access. A one-time statement upload or manual entry feels safer.
*Evidence: P3, P4, P7, P8 vs. P2, P6.*

**Insight 6: Shared and family plans**
A plan owner or parent wants to see who uses what and get money back fairly, because chasing people is awkward. But there's no shared record or reminder, and kids' in-app subscriptions are invisible.
*Evidence: P2, P3, P8.*

**Insight 7: Value for money**
A user paying for many services wants to know which are worth it and whether there's a cheaper option, because they suspect they're overpaying. But comparing bundles and tracking actual usage is too much effort to do alone.
*Evidence: P2, P4, P5.*

## 6. Implications for the Product

| Insight | Implication | Feature in product.md |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Visibility | Dashboard with real monthly/yearly total as the home screen; clear names instead of bank labels | F1 Add manually, F3 Analytics dashboard |
| 2 Trials and yearly renewals | Trial and renewal alerts *before* charging; yearly renewals flagged weeks in advance (e.g. 30 days) | F6 Track free trials, F4 Reminders |
| 3 Cancellation | Per-subscription cancellation help: notice period, deadline reminder, and a pre-filled cancellation letter | F5 Cancellation letters + direct link |
| 4 Price increases | Price history and an alert the moment a price changes | F3 Analytics dashboard (price tracker), F4 Reminders |
| 5 Trust | Manual entry as default; **bank statement upload** (one-time, nothing stored externally) as the auto-detect option; clear privacy explanation | F2 Import bank statement |
| 6 Shared plans | Split cost, track who's paid, group subscriptions by person or family member | F7 Split subscription costs |
| 7 Value | Duplicate detection, savings tips, and a "New offers" screen with cheaper alternatives or bundles | F3 Savings tips, F8 Sponsored offers |

**Implications for the business model**
- Insight 5 shows that trust is fragile: user data can only be monetised in anonymised, aggregated, opt-in form, and no personal or bank data should be kept after import.
- "New offers" (P2, P4) is something users *want*, which makes company-sponsored preference a natural revenue stream that feels less intrusive than ads.
- P6 shows a separate B2B segment (business tools, seats, AI token spend). It's out of scope for the MVP but a long-term opportunity.

**Target user for the MVP:** students and young professionals (P1, P2, P3, P5): the most subscriptions per income, the most forgotten trials, and the most open to the product. Families (P8) and older users (P7) are secondary personas that shape the trust and accessibility requirements.

## 8. Competition and Alternatives

Products are named as reference points; verify the current features before final submission.

| Alternative | What it does | Gap we address |
|---|---|---|
| Bank apps / neobanks (e.g. Revolut, N26) | Show recurring payments from that one account | Only one account; no trials, no cancellation help, no offers |
| Finanzguru (DE) | Detects contracts from bank data, cancellation service | Requires bank connection, which 6 of 8 hesitate about or refuse |
| Bobby / manual tracker apps | Manual list of subscriptions with reminders | No price tracking, no splitting, no cancellation letters |
| Spreadsheets / notebooks (P3, P7) | Self-made overview | Outdated quickly, no reminders |
| Splitwise (P3) | Splits shared costs | Not subscription-aware, no renewal reminders |

## 9. Limitations

- **Synthetic data:** the interviews were simulated from team-defined personas, not conducted with real people. They show plausible patterns but are not evidence of real behaviour.
- **Small, non-representative sample:** 8 participants, mostly from Germany.
- **Next step:** validate Insights 2, 3 and 5 with at least 5 real interviews (fellow students and family members) before the 11.12.2026 deadline, and update this file with real quotes.
